This summary outlines the standard organizational and grading structure of the Liberian secondary education system for the academic year.
I. Academic Year Structure
The Liberian school year is divided into two primary semesters, which are further broken down into six distinct marking periods.
1st Semester:
Marking Period 1 (P1): Initial instruction and continuous assessment.
Marking Period 2 (P2): Continuing instruction and continuous assessment.
Marking Period 3 (P3): Final period for the semester; includes the Period Test and the 1st Semester Exam.
2nd Semester:
Marking Period 4 (P4): Resumption of instruction and continuous assessment.
Marking Period 5 (P5): Continuing instruction and continuous assessment.
Marking Period 6 (P6): Final period for the school year; includes the Period Test and the 2nd Semester Exam.

II. Grading and Assessment
Teachers use a cumulative grading system that combines daily work, periodic tests, and major semester examinations.
Marking Period Grade (CASS): Calculated from assignments, quizzes, and the Period Test given at the end of each of the six periods.
Semester Average: Usually calculated by averaging the grades from the three marking periods (60-70% weight) and combining them with the major Semester Exam (30-40% weight).
Final Yearly Grade: The average of the two final semester grades. A student typically needs a minimum yearly average of 70% per subject to pass.

III. Official Academic Records
Document  Purpose Standard Content
Report Card Internal Progress: Issued to students/parents at the end of each semester or year to track performance. Detailed breakdown of all 6 Marking Periods, all Period Tests, both Semester Exams, and the Final Yearly Average.
Transcript Official Record: Used for university applications, employment, or transferring between schools. A summarized, official document listing final semester/yearly grades for each grade level completed (e.g., 10th, 11th, and 12th grades).
Key Policy Note: Schools are required to provide report cards at the end of the year, while transcripts are generally requested formal documents that may involve an administrative fee
